MAGSEAL — Interactive Feasibility Lab
=====================================

OPENING THE INTERFACE
1. Open MAGSEAL_Interactive_Lab.html directly in Chrome, Safari, or Edge.
2. The program runs entirely offline; no MATLAB installation or local server is necessary.
3. Choose a scenario preset or change settings in the left sidebar.
4. Click "Run simulation" after changing settings.
5. Use Play/Pause, the time slider, and playback speed to inspect the repair sequence.
6. The pressure and leakage graphs compare the chosen scenario with an untreated puncture.
7. "Export CSV" saves the numerical timeline for your own plots or spreadsheet analysis.

WHAT THE INTERFACE USES
- Based on the MAGSEAL repository's simulate.py and parameters.json,
  specifically the version with an EXTERIOR self-healing film and MENP carrier
  delivery on the interior cabin wall.
- A 6 mm reference puncture, 20 m^3 example cabin, 101.325 kPa initial pressure,
  one assumed correctly positioned 200-turn coil at 4 mm standoff.
- Browser-based numerical Biot-Savart circular-coil field; 0.05 s integration step.
- Isothermal compressible orifice leakage, specified exterior-film recovery,
  MENP carrier transport, finite polymer supply, pressure-driven extrusion,
  and prescribed curing kinetics.
- The default 600-s result closely matches the repository's Python calculation:
  approximately 0.67 mg/s final leakage and 56.7 g of air lost.

IMPORTANT SCIENTIFIC LIMITATIONS
- The animated wall and cross-section are schematic representations of calculated
  states, NOT microscope footage, CAD mechanics, or experimentally proven healing.
- Exterior film recovery, its attachment strength, polymer viscosity, supply,
  resin cure behavior, final edge-seal gap and MENP magnetic moment are assumptions.
- A calculated leakage below 1 mg/s is only an illustrative screening criterion.
  It does not demonstrate zero leakage or flight qualification.
- The model does not include actual robotic coil positioning, 3D field errors,
  detailed crack geometry, CFD, gas-polymer interfaces, radiation, thermal cycling,
  or fully coupled structural/contact finite-element analysis.
